tools, certifications — is taken from the existing portfolio pages in this

## Must be filled before sending

Bosch is a tier-1 employer and this programme is heavily oversubscribed. A CV
with unnamed employers or universities will not survive the first screen.

**Blocking — the advert names these as requirements:**

1. **International experience, minimum three months.** The advert states it
   outright. There is nothing in the portfolio that evidences it. If you moved
   to Germany from another country for 42 Heilbronn, that is your answer —
   name the home country and the relocation date. If not, this needs a real
   entry or the application is short of a stated requirement.
2. **German — CEFR level.** The advert asks for "good German skills".
   The portfolio lists German with no level attached.
3. **English — CEFR level.** The advert asks for negotiation level (C1 or above).
4. **Master's grade.** The advert asks for an *outstanding* Master's. Without a
   grade the strongest word in the requirement goes unanswered. Add the grade
   and the scale (e.g. "1.4, German scale" or "3.8/4.0").

**Blocking — credibility:**

5. **University names and locations** for both the MBA and the B.E.
6. **Employer names and locations** for the Operations Manager and Management
   Trainee roles. Unnamed employers read as unverifiable.
7. **Certification years.** The portfolio says 2022–2023 as a block; each
   certificate needs its own year.
8. **Contact block** — address, phone, email, LinkedIn. Also nationality and
   work authorisation for Germany, which German recruiters expect on a CV and
   which removes an early objection if you need a visa.

**Strongly recommended — this is what separates a shortlist from a rejection:**

9. **Numbers on the Operations Manager role.** This is your leadership
   evidence and the advert asks explicitly about human-centric leadership.
   Team size, sites, volumes, and one measurable improvement (on-time
   delivery, error rate, throughput, cost).
10. **Numbers on the freelance consulting.** Client count, sectors, and one
    outcome a client actually got.
11. **Master's thesis title.** If the Retail & E-Commerce project (Jan–May
    2018) was your thesis, say so — it lands directly on supply chain and
    data, which is exactly the target. Right now it sits under Projects
    unlabelled because that link is not stated anywhere in the portfolio.

## Recruiter assessment

**Reads strongly against the advert:**

- MBA in Supply Chain Management is a direct hit on both the degree
  requirement and the domain.
- The data profile is genuinely good. SQL, data modelling, Tableau, Power BI
  and the Google Data Analytics certificate answer "you enjoy understanding
  complex relationships; data, processes and systems are a challenge" with
  evidence rather than adjectives.
- Operations Manager plus Management Trainee means you have already worked
  inside a rotation programme and then run operations. Few applicants to a
  graduate programme have that.
- 42 Heilbronn is a strong differentiator for the digitalisation and
  automation angle. Most SCM applicants cannot read or write code.

**Risks you should go in prepared for:**

- **Programme fit versus your seniority.** The Junior Managers Program is a
  graduate-entry track. Your Master's completed in 2018 and you have close to
  seven years of experience since. Graduate programmes commonly cap how long
  ago the degree was awarded or how much post-graduation experience is
  allowed. I could not verify Bosch's specific rule, so check the eligibility
  criteria on the Bosch careers page before you invest in the application —
  and if there is no hard cap, your cover letter has to explain the choice, or
  a screener will read it as a step backwards.
- **The 42 Heilbronn pivot needs a story.** Moving from supply chain
  operations into C programming looks like a career change unless you frame it
  as deliberate: you went to build the technical depth to lead digital supply
  chain work rather than commission it. Make that explicit in the cover letter.
- **Sustainability is unevidenced.** The advert names digitalisation,
  automation *and* sustainability. You have real evidence for the first two
  and none for the third. Either add something genuine or let the cover letter
  carry it — do not pad the CV with a claim you cannot defend at interview.
- **Two-page length.** Page two currently runs short. It balances once the
  fill-ins are complete. If it still looks thin, that is a signal you are
  under-selling the two experience roles, not a formatting problem.
